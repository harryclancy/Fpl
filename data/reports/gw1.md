## Big picture

Arsenal enter the season as defending champions after ending a 22-year title drought, and open as bookmaker favourites again (approx. +125, approx. 44% implied win probability per one model), with Man City (approx. +333, approx. 20%), Liverpool (approx. 13%), Chelsea (approx. 12%) and Man Utd (approx. 10%) as the chasing pack. Arsenal reportedly beat City comfortably in the Community Shield curtain-raiser — an early form signal, not a fixture result.

An early GW1 "template" is already forming from aggregated manager data (2,000+ squads sampled by one tracker): **Haaland, Bruno Fernandes, João Pedro, Szoboszlai and Rogers** are the most-selected names, with Raya the only defender above 30% ownership. Analysts caution this early ownership is inflated by managers who registered a squad without refining it yet — treat it as a signal, not gospel, until closer to deadline.

## Captaincy consensus

- **Top pick: Haaland** (Man City, home vs Bournemouth) — highest-projected player on the board, already the most-owned player in the pool at roughly 71% (Fantasy Football Scout). Historically FPL's best starter: Haaland has been the top scorer after the first 6 gameweeks in all four of his City seasons, averaging 9 goals and 61.3 points over that stretch.
- **Alternative: Bruno Fernandes** (Man Utd, away at Hull City) — highest-projected midfielder most weeks. Man Utd have arguably the best opening schedule of any side, with back-to-back fixtures against promoted Hull City and Ipswich Town.
- **Differential: Bukayo Saka** (Arsenal, home vs Coventry) — Arsenal have the softest fixture on the opening slate, and Saka's captaincy ownership sits around just 10%, making him a genuine differential armband if you want to break from the Haaland/Bruno consensus.
- **Differential: Gabriel** (Arsenal) — set-piece and clean-sheet upside; mentioned repeatedly across FPL content as a "brave" differential captain given Arsenal's fixture and defensive solidity.
- **Watch: Alexander Isak** (Liverpool) — not yet a top captaincy pick by ownership, but worth knowing: Isak has taken over first-choice penalty duty at Liverpool, and Salah — the long-time taker — is off the list entirely per FPL's own pre-season data. A goal-threat mechanism shift like that is exactly the kind of thing that can turn into a captaincy case within a gameweek or two once it shows up in actual minutes.

## Player notes — why, not just what

The point of this section is the *mechanism*: what specifically changed (an injury, a role/penalty handover, a tactical setup, an opponent's weakness) that's driving the recommendation — not just "good form" or "nice fixture." Every bullet below leads with the causal reason, then who's saying it.

- **Haaland** (Man City, £14.5m-ish) — Captain **because** of a genuine historical pattern, not just current form: he's been Fantasy's top scorer after the first 6 gameweeks in all four of his City seasons (9 goals, 61.3 pts average over that stretch) — City come out fast and he's the beneficiary every year. On top of that, his GW1 fixture (home vs Bournemouth) is soft. Result: The Scout's #1 must-have, 71% ownership already.
- **Bruno Fernandes** (Man Utd) — The captaincy case is a fixture mechanism, not a vibe: United face **two straight promoted sides** (Hull away, then Ipswich at home), historically the softest possible back-to-back for a fixture swing, which is why pundits say they'll be "tripling up" on United attackers those two weeks. Separately, Bruno is explicitly confirmed as United's first-choice on **penalties, free-kicks, and corners** — he's not just a good player in a good moment, he's the guaranteed touchpoint on every dead ball his team wins.
- **Alexander Isak** (Liverpool) — The single biggest under-the-radar mechanic of the whole gameweek: **Isak has taken over first-choice penalty duty at Liverpool, and Salah — Liverpool's penalty taker for most of the last decade — isn't on the list at all.** That's a direct goal-threat transfer from one player to another, confirmed in FPL's own pre-season data, not speculation. If Liverpool win a penalty, it's now Isak's, not Salah's — changes the calculus on both players.
- **João Pedro** (Chelsea, approx. £7.5m) — Nailed for minutes for a specific reason: he was **left out of Brazil's World Cup squad**, which freed up his entire pre-season to train fully under Xabi Alonso instead of reporting back late/tired like international players often do — he scored 8 preseason goals, more than any other FPL-relevant player, on the back of that extra work. Combine that with a friendly £7.5m price (15 goals + 9 assists last season) and it's the value pick of the gameweek. Consensus is explicit though: start him, but the armband should stay on Haaland or Bruno.
- **Gabriel** (Arsenal) — The differential-captain case rests on a specific mechanism: Arsenal's **set-piece routines run through him**, so his goal threat isn't just "defender in a good fixture," it's a guy who scores from a repeatable source (corners/free-kicks) on top of a clean-sheet floor against promoted Coventry.
- **William Saliba (injury) → Gabriel/Raya more essential** — Saliba is out injured, which narrows Arsenal's reliable defensive picks down to Gabriel and Raya specifically — if you're going to Arsenal at the back, pundits say go there, not to the makeshift cover, until Saliba's back.
- **Luke Shaw** (Man Utd, £4.5m) — Same underlying mechanism as the Bruno case: he directly benefits from the Hull-then-Ipswich fixture swing, which is *why* he's the most-owned £4.5m defender (approx. 24%) and explicitly favoured by "elite" managers in tracked data — it's not that he's suddenly a better player, it's that his next two opponents are two of the three weakest in the league.
- **Leif Davis** (Ipswich, £4.0m) — Cheap for a specific reason (relegated club), but he **takes Ipswich's set pieces**, which is the mechanism behind an 8.90 xA last season — the highest of any Championship defender. A £4.0m defender getting assists from corners is a structural, repeatable source of points, not a one-off.
- **Ferdi Kadioglu** (Brighton) — The case here is a minutes/role guarantee that the ownership numbers haven't caught up to yet: 3.2 pts/game over 3,000+ minutes and 34 starts last season, described as "about as nailed as it gets," but sitting at just approx. 3.1% ownership — the underlying reliability exists, the crowd just hasn't noticed.
- **Kai Havertz** — Differential **because** the underlying expected-goals/assists numbers are top-tier but ownership hasn't followed (sub-7%) — the model likes him more than the public does, which is precisely the gap a differential pick needs.
- **Jean-Philippe Mateta** — Same shape as Havertz: strong underlying attacking numbers not yet reflected in ownership, plus a workable fixture — flagged as a season-long hold, not just a GW1 punt.
- **Tarkowski, Maguire, Tavernier** — Grouped together because the reasoning is identical for all three: strong underlying defensive numbers (tackles, blocks, clearances feeding the DEFCON bonus mechanic) that the Shaw/Raya-template crowd is overlooking.
- **Charlie Hughes** (Hull, budget defender) — The mechanism here isn't clean sheets, it's the **DEFCON bonus points system** — cheap defenders from newly-promoted sides who make a lot of defensive actions can bank bonus points most weeks even when they concede, which is why he's flagged as a way to "buy" points at the price floor.
- **Ngumoha** (Liverpool) — Explicitly flagged as boom-or-bust: very low ownership, high variance, no confirmed starting role yet — sites are explicit that this is a punt with an exit plan if he doesn't start, not a set-and-forget pick, which is a different kind of recommendation than everything else on this list.

### Players to be cautious with, and why

- **Newcastle assets generally** — Not one injury but a cluster: Livramento and Joelinton are both listed around 75% fitness, Miley isn't expected back until after GW1, *and* the club is mid-managerial-change following Eddie Howe's departure. Any one of those is a minor concern; all three together is why pundits explicitly say don't stack multiple Newcastle players in the same opening squad.
- **Kudus** (Spurs) — Listed at 75% fitness in the pre-season injury reports — the recommendation to prefer Porro or Solanke instead isn't about their form being better, it's specifically about Kudus's fitness being a question mark this close to deadline.

- Man City host Bournemouth — Haaland's headline fixture.
- Man Utd away at Hull City, then home to Ipswich Town — widely cited as the best two-gameweek fixture swing in the league, driving both the Bruno captaincy case and the Luke Shaw budget-defender case.
